# Card Design Rationale

Per-card notes on *why* each rewrite is shaped the way it is — the Wilson detail
foregrounded, why the options are framed as they are, and what concept the card
introduces. Kept so we can revisit and argue with the choices later.

Voice target: explain the historical situation so the choices are self-evident;
engaging but period; concept explained once on first appearance, linked thereafter
(see scripts/cards/concepts.md). Aftermath = authored consequence + pressure chips;
for the recorded course, ground the consequence in what actually happened.

---

## Phase 1 — Prewar settlement

**card_1555_augsburg_settlement (The Settlement With Gaps)** — Foregrounds the two
unresolved gaps Wilson stresses (the Ecclesiastical Reservation; mediate church
property) rather than narrating 1555 generally, because those gaps are the engine of
the whole war. Options = enforce strictly / treat as living compact / defer — the
three stances toward an ambiguous law. Introduces Augsburg + the Reservation.

**card_1598_styrian_reform (The Styrian Lesson)** — Uses Wilson's concrete Styrian
detail (reform commission 1598, Estates summoned separately, the 800-man garrison,
Brenner the "hammer of heretics", Eisenerz church blown up) so "trust the Styrian
method" is a real, legible choice with a known cost. This is Ferdinand's formative
template; framed retrospectively since it plays among the prewar background cards.

**card_1608_security_blocs (Leagues of Protection)** — Explains *why* the leagues
formed (1608 Diet collapse, courts seizing up) and why Saxony stayed out, so
tolerate/condemn/co-opt are weighted choices. Introduces Union, League, Maximilian.

**card_1609_letter_of_majesty (A Royal Promise in Bohemia)** — Grounds the charter in
Rudolf's weakness and the estates forcing his hand; introduces the Defensors (the
parallel government) and Thurn (with the crown-jewels post that 1617 will revoke), so
later cards can just reference them.

**card_1617_bohemian_enforcement (Before the Window)** — References the Letter of
Majesty + Thurn + Defensors (already introduced) instead of re-explaining; introduces
the Braunau/Klostergrab disputes and Klesl. Sets up the defenestration directly.

## Phase 2 — Bohemian Revolt

General: each recorded course now ends with "In the event…" grounding it in what
actually happened (Wilson), which is both the most compelling aftermath and the
counterfactual anchor. Concepts from Phase 1 are linked, not re-explained.

**card_1618_prague_defenestration (Windows in Prague)** — Opens with the nine-year
Letter-of-Majesty peace and the specific provocations (closed churches, arrests,
royal judges) so the rebels' "evil advisers" framing makes sense; the choice is
riot / lawsuit / war. Introduces the Defenestration as an event-dossier.

**card_1618_remove_klesl (The Cardinal in the Way)** — Frames Klesl as the embodiment
of Matthias's policy of patience, so removing him is removing moderation itself.
Recorded course grounded in the 20 July 1618 seizure to the Tyrol.

**card_1619_stormy_petition (The Storm at Vienna)** — Uses Wilson's vivid scene
(Thurn's 10,000 men, the Hofburg confrontation, the crucifix, Dampierre's cavalry)
so "hold firm" has real jeopardy; recorded course grounded in Thurn's 12 June retreat.

**card_1619_imperial_election / frankfurt_coronation** — Foreground the irony Wilson
stresses: Ferdinand elected emperor unanimously on 28 Aug 1619, the same day Prague
deposed him for Frederick. The two crowns hang together; law vs authority.

**card_1620_bavarian_army (The Bavarian Bargain)** — Centres the Treaty of Munich and
Ferdinand's "Bavarian devil to drive out the Bohemian Beelzebub"; the choice is the
price of an army you don't own. Introduces Munich Treaty + Tilly.

**card_1620_saxon_question** — Built on Wilson's point that John George could be won
by framing the war as law not crusade (and by Lusatia as his price). Introduces him.

**card_1620_march_on_prague (The Road to Prague)** — Ferdinand overruling Bucquoy for
Tilly's bold march; recorded course pays off in White Mountain (8 Nov 1620), which the
card introduces as the battle the whole revolt turns on.
