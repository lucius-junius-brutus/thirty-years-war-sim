# Frederick V of the Palatinate — character design

The "Winter King." A young Calvinist elector who accepts the Bohemian crown (1619),
is crushed at White Mountain (1620), is outlawed, loses the Palatinate and the
electoral dignity (transferred to Bavaria, 1623), and lives out his life as an exile
in The Hague seeking restoration until his death in 1632. His is a tragedy of
overreach. **The player's question: can you do better than history?**

Frederick is deliberately the opposite of Ferdinand — a claimant who loses — chosen to
prove the per-role abstraction holds for a non-emperor whose historical outcome is a
*defeat*. He validated the role-general G1 (the historical line need not be a victory).

## Collapse tiers

- **Terminal:** `dynastic_security` (the house/claim destroyed), `imperial_authority`
  (utterly outlawed with no path back), `military_dependence` (his "army" wholly owns him).
- **Severe:** `estate_trust`, `confessional_legitimacy`, `foreign_intervention_risk`,
  `devastation`, `fiscal_capacity` (he survived broke and abandoned-but-alive).

## Endings — a losing position, so endings are *kinds of defeat* (and rare escapes)

His historical line **collapses** — but into a *specific, authored* defeat, which is his
`historical_outcome`:

- **The Winter King** (dynastic collapse) — *the historical outcome*. Crown lost in a
  winter, the Palatinate overrun, the title given to Bavaria; an exile who keeps the claim
  alive for a son to redeem (Charles Louis, 1648). Somber, not pathetic.
- **Outlawed and Forgotten** (imperial_authority collapse) — the ban makes him a
  non-person in imperial law; the cause dies with his standing.
- **A Pawn Discarded** (foreign + another) — wholly a tool of foreign courts, then dropped.
- **The Adventurer's Ruin** (estate + confessional) — even Protestants disown the reckless
  Calvinist; abandoned by his own side.

Non-failure outcomes (rare, reachable only by skilled non-extreme play):
- **The Crown Held** (ahistorical best case) — consolidates Bohemia; a Protestant kingdom stands.
- **The Elector Who Knew When to Stop** — declines or relinquishes the crown early, keeps the
  Palatinate and dignity intact; the cause is deferred, not destroyed.
- **A Negotiated Survival** — loses Bohemia but bargains to keep lands/title; battered, intact.

**No easy out:** all-bold (seize and fight everything) → catastrophic collapse; all-cautious
(refuse/submit/concede) → an ignominious defeat (loses the cause and standing). The needle is
leveraging his assets — the Union, his foreign in-laws (James I of England), the Protestant
cause — without overreaching into an unwinnable war or capitulating into nothing.

## Initial pressures (1619, at the crown offer)

Strong starting position he will squander: secure elector, head of the Union, royal marriage.
- imperial_authority 60 (a lawful elector, not yet a rebel)
- confessional_legitimacy 64 (Calvinist Union leader, but Lutherans wary)
- estate_trust 55 (Bohemian estates court him; princes uncertain)
- fiscal_capacity 38 (the Palatinate is not rich; Bohemia is bankrupt)
- military_dependence 45 (the Union's army is not really his)
- foreign_intervention_risk 35 (in-laws and Dutch interested, not committed)
- dynastic_security 66 (a secure, prestigious inheritance — for now)
- devastation 12 (peace still holds)

## Deck outline (~24 base cards + conditional crisis cards)

Scope decision: ~22–26 base cards — a full campaign that gives the balance model the
decision density it needs (the gates only tune cleanly with enough divergent decisions),
while keeping the low-agency exile years tight rather than padded.

**The Gamble (1619–20)**
1. **The Crown Offered** — accept the Bohemian crown, decline, or temporize. The hinge.
2. **The Union's Terms** — bind the Protestant Union to the venture, or go without it.
3. **Courting England and the Dutch** — lean on the foreign in-laws; how hard, at what cost to agency.
4. **The Bethlen Alliance** — ally with Bethlen Gábor of Transylvania (a second front) or keep distance.
5. **Lutheran Saxony** — win over / antagonize / ignore John George (the Calvinist–Lutheran rift).
6. **The Coronation in Prague** — rule as a Calvinist zealot (strip Catholic imagery) or conciliate.
7. **Bohemia's Empty Treasury** — fund the war: tax the estates, borrow, or trim ambition.
8. **The Estates' Demands** — concede Bohemian estate privileges to keep their backing, or assert kingship.

**The Unraveling (1620–21)**
9. **Mansfeld and the Freebooters** — rely on mercenary captains who ravage and desert.
10. **The Spanish on the Rhine** — Spinola moves on the Palatinate; defend the homeland or hold Bohemia (not both).
11. **The Eve of White Mountain** — give battle outside Prague, withdraw, or seek terms.
12. **After White Mountain** — flight, a last stand in Prague, or surrender terms.
13. **Silesia and Moravia** — fight on from the peripheries, or let them go.

**Outlawry (1621–23)**
14. **The Imperial Ban** — submit for mercy, defy, or appeal to the electors' constitutional fears.
15. **Defending the Rhine Palatinate** — the Mansfeld/Brunswick campaigns, or abandon the homeland.
16. **Heidelberg Falls** — the Palatine capital lost; fight on or seek a truce.
17. **The Spanish Offer** — a negotiated submission (renounce Bohemia, keep the Palatinate?) — the road not taken.
18. **The Electorate Transferred** — protest, bargain, or accept the loss of the title to Bavaria.

**Exile (1623–32)**
19. **The Court in Exile** — keep a government-in-exile and intrigue, or fade into private life.
20. **The Danish Hope** — pin the cause on Christian IV's intervention.
21. **The Restitution Question** — a chance to bargain for partial restoration on the emperor's terms.
22. **The Swedish Hope** — return in Gustavus Adolphus's train; a pawn's last chance.
23. **Frederick and Gustavus** — terms with the Swede: restoration as a client king, or hold out for independence.
24. **Death at Mainz (1632)** — the epilogue; what the claim passes to his heir, Charles Louis.

Each card: both-sides effects (G3), exactly one `historical_option`, Wilson-grounded, dossiers
for links. Tune with `probe-strategies`/`check-character` until the historical line lands on
**The Winter King** (G1) and both extremes collapse into their own defeats (G2).
